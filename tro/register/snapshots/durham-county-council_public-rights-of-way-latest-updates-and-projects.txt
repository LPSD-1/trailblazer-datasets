Page Bank Footbridge closure
The Weardale Way on the north bank of the River Wear, between Page Bank and the bridleway near Holywell Hall, is closed due to a dangerous bridge over Brancepeth Beck. Unfortunately, there is no direct alternative route on either bank of the river without using sections of road.
View Footpath 104, Greater Willington, Footpath 12, Brancepeth Parish, Footpath 45, Spennymoor Parish and Footpath 107 Brandon and Byshottles Parish, Page Bank Footbridge for more information.
Celebrating 60 years of the Pennine Way
The Pennine Way celebrated its 60th anniversary in 2025, and a touring exhibition visited Bowlees Visitor Centre in Teesdale. That exhibition can now be enjoyed online at National Trails: A Trail of Inspiration (The Pennine Way 60th anniversary exhibition).
Durham City Green Corridor project
We have undertaken a range of access improvement works recently as part of the Durham City Green Corridor project. This partnership programme is dedicated to enhancing accessible green spaces close to the heart of the city. The work has been funded by the Rights of Way Improvement Plan and the National Trust. Our goal is to make it easier for everyone to reconnect with nature, the city, and each other.
Our teams have been hard at work improving our beautiful woodlands and green spaces! Here's what we've accomplished so far:
Frankland Wood: surfacing and fencing along the access track.
Around Kepier Farm: replacement of old stiles with kissing gates and step repairs.
Cocken Wood: Repaired metal fencing and concrete steps.
Kepier Wood: Installed new fencing around the car park, new signage in the woods.
Mallygill and Raintonpark Woods: new stiles and step repairs.
New timber finger posts across the whole area.
Improved footpath access thanks to local collaboration
The Public Rights of Way (PROW) team recently partnered with a local farmer to enhance access along a popular footpath while ensuring the safety of livestock in the adjacent field.
The farmer had been facing ongoing issues with the field gate being left open by walkers, resulting in sheep escaping onto the nearby road. Additionally, the stile next to the gate had become damaged and unsafe for use.
To resolve these concerns, PROW provided a new kissing gate, which the farmer installed alongside repairs to the adjoining fence. This improvement not only secures the field for livestock but also offers safer and more reliable access for walkers.
